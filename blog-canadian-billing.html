Billing OHIP, MSP and Private Insurance: A Practical Guide for Canadian Practices

  • Rahul Khinchi
  • Nov 15, 2026
  • 6 min read

Billing in Canada works differently from the US. Most physician services are billed to a provincial health plan, while many allied health and extended services are billed to private insurers or paid by the patient. Each province has its own rules, systems and deadlines, so a process that works in Ontario will not carry over to British Columbia as it is.

This guide covers the basics of billing the two largest provincial plans, OHIP and MSP, and private extended health insurance.

OHIP (Ontario)

The Ontario Health Insurance Plan (OHIP) pays for insured physician services in Ontario. Claims are coded using the OHIP Schedule of Benefits and submitted electronically to the Ministry of Health.

  • Submission: claims are sent electronically, usually through your EMR or billing software.
  • Deadline: for services provided on or after April 1, 2023, OHIP claims must be submitted within three months of the date of service. This was shortened from six months, so older internal checklists may be out of date.
  • Reconciliation: the ministry returns error reports for claims that could not be processed and a monthly remittance advice showing what was paid. Both need to be reviewed and acted on.
  • Common problems: invalid or expired health card numbers, missing version codes, fee codes that do not match the diagnosis, and services that need a referring physician number.

MSP (British Columbia)

The Medical Services Plan (MSP) covers insured physician services in British Columbia. Claims are submitted electronically through Teleplan, the province's billing system, and coded using the MSC Payment Schedule.

  • Submission: through Teleplan, usually via your EMR or a billing service.
  • Deadline: MSP claims should be submitted within 90 days of the date of service. Claims sent later need a special submission code and can be refused.
  • Reconciliation: remittance reports show paid, adjusted and refused claims. Refusals need to be corrected and resubmitted before the deadline.
  • Common problems: incorrect personal health numbers, eligibility issues, missing referral information and fee items billed outside their rules.

Private extended health insurance

Services that provincial plans do not cover, such as many physiotherapy, chiropractic, massage therapy, mental health and dental services, are often billed to private insurers through a patient's group or individual benefits plan.

  • Direct billing: many clinics submit claims electronically to insurers on the patient's behalf through insurer portals or claims networks.
  • Coverage checks: plans vary widely in what they cover, annual maximums and whether a doctor's referral is required. Check before the first visit.
  • Patient balances: anything the plan does not pay becomes the patient's responsibility, so explain this clearly at booking.

Privacy rules are different too

US rules such as HIPAA do not apply to Canadian patient information. Canadian practices are governed by provincial health privacy laws, such as Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA), and by federal law where it applies. Any billing partner should be able to explain how they meet the rules in your province, including where patient data is accessed and stored.

Steps that reduce rejected claims

  1. Validate health card numbers and version codes at every visit.
  2. Submit claims weekly or more often, well inside provincial deadlines.
  3. Review every error report and remittance advice, and correct rejected claims promptly.
  4. Keep fee codes and payment schedules up to date, as they change regularly.
  5. Check private coverage and referral requirements before the first appointment.

Rules, fee codes and deadlines change. Always confirm current requirements in your province's latest billing guide or schedule of benefits.
